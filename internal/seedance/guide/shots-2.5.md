## Shots and timeline (Seedance 2.5 series)

Divide the video with timestamps or "Shot N"; either is fine, and they combine well: "Shot 1 (0-4s): ...". Describe each segment's visuals, camera movement, actions, dialogue and sound.

- Use 1-second intervals as the basic unit. Keep the timeline continuous with no gaps (never "0-3s ... 5-6s").
- Too little plot in a time range makes the model improvise; too much causes excessive cuts or dropped plot. Allocate content to match the seconds.
- Do not use timestamps for high-frequency actions ("shake your head three times per second").
- Time points and relative time also work: "At the 5-second mark, a quick left wipe", "After 3 seconds, everyone shakes their head", "The frame freezes for 1 second after she presses the shutter".
- For a transition, state both the trigger point and the method: "At the 5-second mark, the camera quickly transitions leftward using a left wipe combined with a natural dissolve."
- A shot header may carry its framing in brackets: "Shot 2: [Medium shot, over-the-shoulder shot] ...".
