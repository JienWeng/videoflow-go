## Shots (Seedance 2.0 series)

The ideal form for anything beyond one simple action is a timeline-based storyboard: break the video into shots and describe each in the order events occur: who + where + doing what + how the camera moves.

- Label them "Shot 1:", "Shot 2:", "Shot 3:" (primary events first).
- Do NOT put second counts or timestamps on shots. The 2.0 series handles precise timing (such as 0-3 seconds) unstably, and forcing durations can break the result. Let the model pace each shot from the plot. Give a longer shot more to show, a shorter one less.
- Organise each shot as: camera movement or transition ("slowly push in from a wide shot", "fixed camera position", "cut to ..."), then the subject's key actions and changes of expression, then position or spatial change, then audio (sound effects, voices, music for that shot).

Bad: "A man runs nervously down the street, and the scene feels very cinematic."
Good:
Shot 1: Side shot of a street alley; the man slowly starts running, with a sense of rapid breathing.
Shot 2: The man knocks over a fruit stand; the camera shakes quickly and gives a close-up of the man's frightened face.
Shot 3: The man climbs over a low wall and disappears; the camera slowly pulls back and freezes on the empty street.
