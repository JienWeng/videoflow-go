## Action and emotion

- Refine body movement and quantify degree: name the body part (hands, legs, head, shoulders, back) and add range, speed and force: "slowly raise a hand", "quickly turn the head", "slightly lower the head".
- Prioritise slow, gentle, continuous small movements: "walk slowly", "gently raise a hand", "sit down naturally". Avoid high-burst, large actions such as sprinting, big jumps and violent rolls.
- Supply the transition between actions so motion is coherent: "use the inertia of turning around to naturally raise a hand", "naturally transition from a pause into raising a hand".
- Give general descriptions priority and write specific detail only for a few memorable actions. Do not repeat the same action.
- Externalise emotion as concrete physical detail instead of abstract words such as "very sad" or "extremely angry":
  - Sadness: lowering the head, shoulders trembling slightly, eyes reddening, fingers clutching the corner of clothing, tears welling but not falling.
  - Joy: corners of the mouth rising, brows and eyes relaxing, steps becoming light.
  - Nervousness: checking the watch, fingers tapping the tabletop, rapid breathing, eyes darting away.
  - Anger: fists clenched, jawline tense, chest heaving, words squeezed out through gritted teeth.
  - Relief: a long breath out, tense shoulders relaxing, a faint smile, looking up into the distance.
- Use descriptive sentences and few idioms. Keep emotional words mild ("amazed", not "fanatical" or "extremely shocked"): strong emotional cues can make eyes glow unnaturally.
