## Spoken lines (Seedance 2.0 series)

- Put each spoken line in curly braces, inside the shot where it is spoken, right after who says it and how: `She raises her head, unable to hold back a smile, and says {I was kidding}`.
- For narration write it as a voice-over with the voice described first: `Voiceover: a deep, serene male voice says {In the vast silence of the cosmos...}`.
- Describe the voice itself, in a few words of timbre and delivery: "a low, thick, warm, finely grainy middle-aged male voice", "a light, upbeat tone", "with a playful tone". This is what makes a voice sound human instead of flat. Derive it from the character and the mood of the story. Keep one narrator's voice description identical across the clip.
- Write every line exactly once, word for word as given. Never paraphrase, translate, shorten or repeat a line, and never restate its words elsewhere in the prompt.
- The language of dialogue must be consistent; do not mix languages (proper nouns excepted). If the language is neither Chinese nor English, mark it: `says in Japanese {こんにちは}`.
- Give speech room: a calm, unhurried pace, a short pause between sentences, and the last line ending before the video ends. When a video has narration, abrupt clicks and cut-off noise are likely at the very end, so never let a line run to the last moment.
- For a narrator nobody on screen moves their lips. For characters talking, the speaker is visible, facing enough toward the camera, and their lips match the words.
